# PortWay SI — Knowledge Engine & Community Growth

## Product identity
PortWay SI is a strategic intelligence and research literacy workspace. It helps users organize evidence, understand systems, compare scenarios, and track change. It does not claim omniscience or provide guaranteed investment forecasts.

## Strict data boundary
- PortWay SI owns its own document store, vector index, source registry, research notes, prompts, AI configuration, and analytics namespace.
- Never query or import Health SI medical records, health questions, embeddings, or private user data.
- A shared Pi membership service may provide an opaque account-link reference only. Do not send PortWay research activity or private notes to Health SI.
- Separate access permissions for public, member-only, contributor, reviewer, and administrator content.

## Evidence record format
Each record should contain:
- id, title, summary, full_text, domain, tags
- publisher, author, primary_source_url, secondary_sources
- published_at, effective_at, retrieved_at, last_checked_at
- jurisdiction, entities, claims, evidence_type
- status: verified_primary / corroborated / unverified_claim / inference / disputed / outdated
- confidence_rationale, counter_evidence, unknowns
- version, reviewer, review_status, content_license_or_reuse_basis

A press release proves that an announcement was made; it does not by itself prove implementation, regulatory approval, integration, custody, liquidity, or on-chain activity. Track those as separate claims.

## RAG response policy
- Retrieve only PortWay SI records approved for the requested audience.
- Cite source titles, URLs, dates, and exact claim coverage.
- Separate facts, unverified claims, inference, and unknowns.
- For laws, distinguish proposal, passage, signature, effective date, and implementation.
- For partnerships, distinguish announcement, contract, technical integration, production use, and measurable activity.
- For tokenized assets, separately verify issuer, legal rights, custodian, underlying asset, audit, redemption, and on-chain evidence.
- If current verification is required but no current source is available, state that limitation.
- Never use Health SI documents as fallback context.

## Daily return loop
- Global Intelligence Brief: 3–5 material developments with source and timestamp.
- One-minute concept: explain one complex system in plain Vietnamese.
- Claim-check: one popular claim labeled with evidence status.
- Strategic map: show actors, incentives, dependencies, and bottlenecks.
- Weekly scenario: base case, alternatives, assumptions, and indicators to monitor.
- Contributor desk: submit a primary source, timeline update, or evidence-backed analysis.

## Sharing and responsible virality
Each share card should contain one insight, a source/date, a status label, and a link back to the full dossier. Use curiosity and clarity, not sensationalism. Never turn an inference into a confirmed headline, manufacture urgency, promise token prices, or reward spam.

Suggested share actions: share a verified brief, challenge a claim with evidence, submit an official document, or invite a peer to a research challenge. Reward only accepted, useful contributions or verified paid activity under a published budget and anti-abuse policy.

## Content cadence
- Daily: 1 intelligence brief, 1 concept explainer, 1 claim-check.
- Weekly: 1 deep research dossier, 1 scenario map, 1 contributor roundup.
- Monthly: source audit, stale-claim review, jurisdiction update, and correction report.

## Quality workflow
Ingest → deduplicate → extract claims → locate primary sources → corroborate → classify status → editorial review → publish → monitor changes → archive/version.
Keep original source snapshots or hashes where permitted. Respect copyright, licenses, robots/access terms, and privacy. Do not republish full copyrighted articles.


## Flagship specialization: Gành Rái Maritime Economic Intelligence

### Product thesis
PortWay SI's flagship research program is the connected maritime economy around the Gulf of Gành Rái. It is an independent analytical lens, not an approved regional master plan, legal administrative unit, or governance authority. The research connects Cái Mép–Thị Vải, Cái Mép Hạ, Cần Giờ, Phú Mỹ–Long Sơn, and Vũng Tàu as linked but distinct nodes.

The goal is to understand how ports, shipping, cargo owners, industrial production, energy, logistics, urban development, marine services, capital, regulation, and ecological systems interact. Do not assume that all nodes are complementary: explicitly test competition, duplication, dependencies, capacity constraints, and environmental trade-offs.

### Geographic dossiers

#### 1. Cái Mép – Thị Vải – Cái Mép Hạ
- Port and terminal map: legal owner, investor, operator, terminal type, berth, channel/depth claims, equipment, designed capacity, actual throughput, and operating status.
- Shipping network: shipping lines, services, destinations, vessel classes, calls, transshipment/direct-call distinctions, and schedule reliability.
- Cargo intelligence: container, bulk, liquid, project cargo; import/export direction; commodity groups; hinterland origin/destination; seasonality.
- Port competition and complementarity: terminal overlap, specialization, capacity utilization, pricing evidence, and actual cargo capture.
- Hinterland: roads, waterways, ICDs, depots, warehouses, customs, trucking, barge services, and door-to-port cost/time where data exists.

#### 2. Long Sơn – Phú Mỹ
- Industrial and energy asset map: legal entity, owner, operator, facility, approved scope, construction status, commissioning, and operating status.
- Petrochemical, energy, industrial inputs/outputs, industrial services, liquid/bulk cargo, and dedicated port interfaces.
- Utilities and dependencies: electricity, gas, water, feedstock, storage, waste treatment, safety, and emergency response.
- Trace inbound raw materials and outbound products to markets and port/logistics corridors.
- Separate announced investment, approved investment, registered capital, disbursed capital, construction, and operating assets.

#### 3. Cần Giờ
- Port and transport proposals: record proposal, planning inclusion, approval, permits, financing, construction, and operation as distinct states.
- Maritime access, navigation channels, vessel traffic interactions, road/bridge/ferry connections, and logistics interface with Cái Mép.
- Urban development, tourism, marine services, livelihoods, and local community impacts.
- Mangrove forest, buffer zones, biodiversity, water quality, sediment, emissions, climate risks, and ecological carrying capacity.
- Treat conservation and environmental compliance as core feasibility conditions, not an afterthought. Do not equate a proposal or investment announcement with an operating port.

#### 4. Regional connectors
Include Phú Mỹ, Vũng Tàu, Cái Mép–Thị Vải, Cái Mép Hạ and other relevant hinterland nodes when they materially affect flows, capacity, labor, energy, housing, services, or connectivity. Preserve each node's own legal geography and administrative status; do not flatten them into one jurisdiction.

### Eight cross-region intelligence layers
1. Port ownership, operators, terminals, shipping lines, vessels, and maritime services.
2. Cargo flows, commodity groups, import/export markets, cargo owners, and customers.
3. Industrial clusters, petrochemicals, energy, utilities, and supply-chain dependencies.
4. Hinterland transport: road, waterway, channel, bridge, rail proposals, ICD, depot, warehouse, and customs.
5. Planning, law, permits, public investment, PPP, private capital, financing, and project delivery milestones.
6. Blue economy, mangroves, biodiversity, emissions, climate resilience, and environmental carrying capacity.
7. Digital trade: port community systems, electronic documents, cargo traceability, insurance, payments, and trade finance.
8. Scenario analysis: regional complementarity/competition, sequencing, bottlenecks, cargo demand, and leading indicators.

### Required project and asset record
Each named port, industrial facility, infrastructure project, or development proposal should have: canonical name and aliases; coordinates/administrative location where verified; project owner and legal entity; operator; project type; source register; planning/legal status; investment status; construction/operation status; design capacity; actual throughput/output; timeline; dependencies; risks; environmental constraints; related nodes; last checked date; reviewer; and confidence rationale.

### Cargo-flow model
Model flows as origin → commodity → shipper/consignee → mode → terminal/port → destination market, with date range and source. Distinguish observed flow data from estimates and scenario assumptions. Never infer real cargo volumes from designed port capacity, announced investment, vessel capacity, or promotional materials.

### Source hierarchy and verification
Prioritize official planning and legal documents, maritime/port authorities, customs and statistical agencies, environmental impact and monitoring documents, audited company reports, exchange disclosures, operator/terminal records, and original technical documents. Use credible media and industry databases for leads and corroboration, not as substitutes for primary records when legal or operational status is at issue.

For every claim, store source publication date, effective date if relevant, retrieval date, geographic scope, exact claim supported, and evidence status. Separate: proposal; planning inclusion; investment approval; permit; financing; construction; commissioning; commercial operation. For throughput, distinguish design capacity from actual annual handled volume. For investment, distinguish announced, registered, approved, disbursed, and completed capital.

### Strategic outputs
- Regional node map: who owns, operates, connects, supplies, and depends on each node.
- Port and shipping atlas: terminals, routes, services, vessels, and hinterland links.
- Cargo-flow dashboard: observed cargo and clearly labeled estimates.
- Project tracker: legal and delivery milestones with source-backed change history.
- Infrastructure gap map: bottlenecks, missing links, redundancy, and capacity constraints.
- Blue-economy risk dashboard: mangrove, biodiversity, water, emissions, climate and compliance indicators.
- Investment landscape: public/private actors, financing instruments, PPP opportunities, and disclosed project milestones; no guaranteed returns.
- Scenario lab: base/upside/downside scenarios, assumptions, dependencies, leading indicators, and disconfirming evidence.
- Business opportunity briefs: logistics services, warehousing, maritime services, digital documentation, cargo visibility, insurance and trade finance, each with demand evidence and regulatory constraints.

### Research questions PortWay should answer
- Which terminals actually handle which cargo, for which markets, and with what service pattern?
- Where do Cái Mép, Cái Mép Hạ and a proposed Cần Giờ port compete, and where could they complement one another?
- What industrial cargo demand is generated by Long Sơn–Phú Mỹ, and which port/logistics services can serve it?
- Which road, waterway, channel, bridge, warehouse, ICD or customs constraints materially raise cost/time?
- Which project milestones are legally approved, funded, under construction, or operational today?
- What are the ecological limits and environmental obligations around Cần Giờ and the wider bay?
- What services or digital infrastructure can create measurable value without assuming speculative tokenization?

### Scope guardrails
- Keep PortWay SI independent from InterLink and do not make InterLink a core product dependency.
- Do not force Pi Network or SidraChain into the regional thesis. Evaluate any future technology only as an optional infrastructure layer with a specific use case, legal fit, security model, and measurable benefit.
- Do not label Gành Rái GBA as an officially approved regional authority or master plan.
- Never invent port capacity, cargo volumes, project status, ownership, investment, permits, or ecological impacts.
- Treat Cần Giờ's mangrove ecosystem and environmental constraints as central decision variables.
